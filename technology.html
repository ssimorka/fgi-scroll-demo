First Genomic Insights · Non-invasive fetal sequencing

Our technology

The platform behind non-invasive fetal sequencing.

NIFS pairs a single maternal blood draw with FirstHelix®, our patented bioinformatic engine, to screen the fetal exome for thousands of genetic conditions, non-invasively.

How it works Get in touch

The problem, and our solution

Today's prenatal tests often trade breadth against risk. NIFS was developed to broaden what a blood test can reveal.

Standard NIPT has a limited, defined scope

Current screening with NIPT looks for a specific set of conditions. Many genetic disorders are outside its scope.

Invasive testing carries real risk

Diagnostic testing with whole exome sequencing can look more broadly but requires an invasive procedure such as an amniocentesis or chorionic villus sampling (CVS).

NIFS is non-invasive and designed for broad exome coverage

A new class of prenatal screening.

NIFS is designed to screen the fetal exome from a single maternal blood draw, delivering high-resolution genetic information that until now required an invasive procedure.

How it works

A simple blood draw. A sophisticated pipeline.

A defined wet-lab workflow with advanced bioinformatics, built for laboratory deployment.

01 · Sample

High-resolution NIFS starts with maternal plasma, which carries cell-free DNA (cfDNA) from both the mother and the fetus (placenta). Fragments of cfDNA that are fetal in origin tend to be shorter than those of maternal origin.

02 · Capture and sequencing

Rather than targeting a small panel of loci, the assay enriches the protein-coding regions of ~23,000 genes directly from cfDNA and sequences them at high depth. Every site is read from a mixture of maternal and fetal molecules, so the next step is telling the two apart.

Technology covered by issued and pending patents, exclusively licensed to FGI.

03 · Deconvolution

At every site, the fraction of reads carrying a variant depends on the specific combination of maternal and fetal genotypes, and on the fetal fraction. Our FirstHelix® bioinformatics engine uses machine learning to determine the fetal genotype based on that variant read fraction, as well as other features of the reads including fragment size.

04 · Test modules

Because the whole exome is captured from a single cfDNA sample, one dataset supports several result types: chromosome-level aneuploidies, copy-number variants, and sequence variants interpreted under dominant and recessive models. FirstHelix® infers fetal sex from the same data, which anchors interpretation of X-linked findings.

By the numbers

Why the exome?

Most known genetic disease starts in a small part of the genome. The exome is the roughly 1–2% of the genome that codes for proteins. It’s also where most known disease-causing variants are found. That’s why exome sequencing is used to evaluate a wide range of single-gene conditions.

It has not been cleared or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration or any other regulatory authority. FGI does not offer testing directly to patients or healthcare providers. If NIFS becomes available, independent laboratory partners will offer it, subject to applicable laboratory and regulatory requirements in each market.
